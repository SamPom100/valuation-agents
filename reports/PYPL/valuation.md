# PYPL — PayPal Holdings, Inc. | DCF Valuation

**Date:** 2026-04-20 · **Price:** $50.98 · **Market cap:** $47B · **Shares outstanding:** 900M

---

## 1. Company Overview

PayPal Holdings operates a two-sided digital payments platform (branded PayPal checkout, Venmo P2P, Braintree unbranded processing, Xoom remittance) connecting ~435M consumer and merchant accounts across >200 markets. Its economic engine is a transaction take-rate on total payment volume (TPV) running roughly $1.6T annually, monetized through transaction fees net of processing and funding costs. Since spinning out from eBay in 2015, PayPal has evolved from a closed-loop checkout monopoly into a branded-checkout business facing structural competition from Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay, and bank-rail incumbents (Zelle, FedNow). 2024–2025 was a transition period under CEO Alex Chriss: management pivoted from growth-at-any-cost to disciplined capital allocation, sharply slowed headcount, and launched a Braintree re-pricing initiative. Revenue growth has decelerated from low-double-digits (2020–2022) to ~4% (2025), while net income and FCFF have held up, producing a cash-machine business at a depressed multiple.

### Payout ratio

FY25: Dividends $130M + Buybacks $6,052M = $6,182M on $5,233M net income → **118%** (unsustainable — eating into cash).

5-yr cumulative (2021–2025): Dividends $130M + Buybacks $24,673M = $24,803M on $20,214M net income → **123%**.

PayPal has been aggressively returning >100% of earnings because it started with a huge cash cushion and strong OCF. For DCF purposes, an 80% payout ratio is the realistic steady-state — still very shareholder-friendly but not drawing down cash. Use **80%** across all scenarios.

## 5. Reverse DCF — What Is the Market Pricing In?

```

>>> Implied Starting Growth: 2.50% (fading to 2.5%)

```

The reverse DCF hits the **floor** — at a 15× terminal P/E, even zero growth (terminal-only growth of 2.5%) produces an intrinsic value of **$73.72**, which is **45% above** the current $50.98 price. The market is implying EITHER:

- A terminal P/E below 10× (i.e., permanent decline / structural impairment), or
- Growth permanently below the risk-free rate (i.e., the business is a melting ice cube), or
- A discount rate materially higher than 9.28% (i.e., the market is pricing extra idiosyncratic risk).

Any reasonable combination of these inputs produces a harsh conclusion: the market has priced PYPL as a run-off business. That is an aggressive interpretation relative to the 2025 fundamentals (revenue +4.3%, operating margin +160bps, NI +26%, $6B of buybacks retiring ~7% of shares).

## 12. Verdict

### **UNDERVALUED — HIGH CONVICTION**

- Current price $50.98 vs probability-weighted fair value $98.61 → **93% upside**.
- **Every cell** of the 25-cell EPS DCF sensitivity table is above the current price. The bear-case scenario (4% growth, 10× P/E — i.e., current narrative becomes permanent) still yields **$63.20** (+24%).
- The market is priced as if PYPL is a melting ice cube (reverse DCF implies growth BELOW the risk-free rate), yet 2025 delivered +4.3% revenue, +260bps operating margin, +26% NI, and $6B of buybacks that retired ~7% of shares.
- ROIC of 21% vs 9.3% WACC — this is a franchise, not a commodity business, trading at 10× earnings.
- Terminal multiple is the single most important input. If PayPal's brand and network effects persist (base 15×), upside is material. If they don't (bear 10×), you still make money from capital returns alone.

### Upside risks (i.e., why the stock could work)
1. **Multiple re-rating**: At median historical P/E (31.5×), PYPL would be worth ~$170 on current EPS — no growth required.
2. **Aggressive buybacks**: At current prices, $6B/yr repurchases retire ~12%/yr of share count, mechanically growing EPS even on flat NI.
3. **Braintree re-pricing**: FY25 operating margin expansion suggests the unbranded-processing repricing is sticking — could drive further margin upside.
4. **Venmo monetization**: Take-rate inflection as Venmo gets embedded into commerce (Amazon, DoorDash).

### Downside risks (i.e., why it could stay cheap / get cheaper)
1. **Permanent share loss in checkout**: Apple Pay / Shop Pay could compound gains, compressing TPV growth and take-rate simultaneously.
2. **Stable-coin disruption**: If stablecoin rails materially lower the cost of digital payments, PYPL's take-rate comes under structural pressure.
3. **Tax rate normalization**: 2025's 16.8% ETR flatters EPS by ~$0.35/share; the next couple years likely normalize toward 22%, masking underlying growth.
4. **Value-trap risk**: Stock has been "cheap" for 3 years; a re-rating requires a catalyst the market can see, not just fundamental cash generation.

### Conviction: **HIGH**

The margin of safety at these levels is unusual. A 24% floor (bear case) with a 93% central-case upside and limited downside (franchise economics + buyback support) is an asymmetric risk/reward. The primary risk is time — the stock may remain cheap — but capital returns alone (8–10%/yr in buyback yield) compensate for waiting.
