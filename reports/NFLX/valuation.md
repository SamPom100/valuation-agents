## 1. Company Overview

Netflix is the world's dominant streaming entertainment platform with 300M+ paid subscribers across 190+ countries. The company produces and licenses content across films, series, documentaries, and games, monetized through subscriptions and a growing ad-supported tier. Netflix has completed its transition from cash-burning growth to a highly profitable, capital-returning business — operating margins expanded from 4.3% (2016) to 29.5% (2025), and the company returned $9.1B via buybacks in FY2025. Revenue grew from $8.8B (2016) to $45.2B (2025), a ~20% CAGR. Note: NFLX completed a 10:1 stock split; all per-share figures are post-split.

```
CAPM COST OF EQUITY

  Risk-free rate: 4.38% (10Y Treasury)
  Beta: 1.71
```

CAPM of 11.01% is used for the EPS DCF (cost of equity for equity cash flows). WACC of 10.78% is used for the Revenue DCF (cost of capital for firm-level cash flows). The two are nearly identical because Netflix carries minimal financial leverage (3.5% debt weighting).

**Narrative justification:**
- **Bear (12% EPS / 10% revenue):** Subscriber saturation in developed markets, content cost inflation from competing studios, ad-tier underperforms. Growth decelerates faster than expected. Market re-rates to 28x as growth premium fades.

- **Bull (25% EPS / 20% revenue):** Ad-tier becomes a major revenue driver, gaming monetization takes off, live events (sports) drive subscriber additions. Netflix becomes a broader entertainment platform. Market rewards with 45x multiple.

**Cross-method divergence note:** The EPS DCF consistently values NFLX higher than the Revenue DCF (base: $110.85 vs $51.51). This ~2x gap arises because:
1. The EPS DCF terminal value ($251.69 at 37x P/E) implicitly prices in long-run growth beyond Year 10, while the Revenue DCF's Gordon Growth terminal is bounded by FCFF margins
2. Netflix's FCFF margin (20.9%) understates earnings power — operating CF already absorbs content investment, but the margin is suppressed by working capital swings and content timing
3. ROIC of 35.9% means each dollar reinvested creates ~$3.60 in value, which P/E multiples capture but FCFF margins do not

The EPS DCF is weighted more heavily because Netflix's primary value driver is earnings compounding (content creates moats that sustain returns), and the exit-multiple approach better reflects the market's willingness to pay for franchise value.

## 12. Verdict: FAIRLY VALUED

**Fair Value: ~$91 | Current Price: $92.67 | Upside/Downside: -1.5%**

Netflix is priced almost exactly at our probability-weighted fair value. The market is efficiently pricing in ~14% EPS growth fading to 3%, which is a reasonable expectation for a company transitioning from hyper-growth to mature-growth. The base case EPS DCF suggests ~20% upside, but this is offset by the Revenue DCF's more conservative cash-flow-grounded value. At 37x trailing earnings and 8.7x revenue, Netflix is pricing in excellent execution — there is limited margin of safety.

**Upside risks:**
- Ad-tier monetization exceeds expectations, driving ARPU materially above subscription-only economics
- Live sports/events (NFL, WWE, boxing) create a new growth vector and reduce churn
- Gaming becomes a meaningful revenue contributor, leveraging the content library IP

**Downside risks:**
- Subscriber growth stalls in key markets as penetration nears saturation (~250M households globally with broadband)
- Content cost inflation accelerates as studios retain more content and compete aggressively for talent
- Regulatory risk: antitrust scrutiny of market dominance, content regulations in international markets

**Conviction: Medium.** The two DCF methods diverge significantly (EPS DCF is 2x Revenue DCF in base case), reducing confidence in any single point estimate. The blended approach mitigates this, but the wide range ($57-$134 across scenarios) reflects genuine uncertainty about Netflix's growth trajectory and terminal valuation.
