## 1. Company Overview

Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) is the dominant provider of credit scores in the United States, used in approximately 90% of US lending decisions. The FICO Score is deeply embedded in financial system infrastructure — from mortgages to credit cards to auto loans — creating extraordinary pricing power and near-monopoly economics. The company also operates a Software business (analytics, fraud detection, decision management) that contributes roughly 40% of revenue. Over the past decade, FICO has aggressively monetized its scoring franchise through price increases, driving revenue from $881M (FY2016) to $1,991M (FY2025) — a 9.5% CAGR — while expanding operating margins from 19.2% to 46.5%. ROIC of 67% confirms this is one of the highest-quality business franchises in public markets.

Revenue DCF uses SBC-adjusted margin (30.5%) as starting point given capex/D&A > 2x. SBC at $157M (7.9% of revenue) is a real economic cost — diluted shares declined from 32.3K to 24.6K over 10 years, but this was funded primarily by debt, not organic cash flow.

**Payout Ratio:**
- FY2025: $1,415M buybacks / $652M NI = **217%** (unsustainable — debt-funded)
- FCFF SBC-adj / NI = $607 / $652 = **93%** → use **90%** in model (normalized sustainable payout)

**Net Debt:** $3,056M total debt - $189M cash & investments = **$2,867M**
- Long-term debt: $2,656M + Current maturities: $400M = $3,056M
- Cash: $134M + Marketable securities: $55M = $189M

**ROIC:**
- NOPAT = $925M × (1 - 0.188) = $751M
- Invested Capital = (-$1,746M equity) + $3,056M debt - $189M cash = $1,121M
- **ROIC = 67.0%** (>> 9.32% CAPM — every dollar of growth creates enormous value)

**WACC (for Revenue DCF):**
- Pre-tax cost of debt = $134M interest / $3,056M debt = 4.37%
- After-tax cost of debt = 4.37% × (1 - 0.188) = 3.55%
- E/(E+D) = $25,000M / $28,215M = 88.6%; D/(E+D) = 11.4%
- **WACC = 88.6% × 9.32% + 11.4% × 3.55% = 8.66%**

**Cross-method divergence note:** The EPS DCF consistently values FICO much higher than the Revenue DCF (base: $1,284 vs $552 — a 132% gap). This is driven by three factors:
1. **Debt-funded buybacks**: FICO has spent $6.6B on buybacks over 10 years, largely funded by $3.1B in debt. This artificially accelerates EPS growth (fewer shares) which the EPS DCF values, while the Revenue DCF penalizes the resulting $2.9B net debt subtracted from enterprise value.
2. **SBC deduction**: The Revenue DCF uses SBC-adjusted FCFF margins (30.5%), treating $157M/year in SBC as a real cost. The EPS DCF captures SBC only indirectly through dilution.
3. **Terminal value methodology**: The EPS DCF's exit multiple (30-55x P/E) implicitly assigns franchise value, while the Revenue DCF's Gordon Growth terminal is bounded by FCFF margins and WACC.

The EPS DCF is weighted more heavily (70%) because FICO's value is primarily its monopoly franchise — an EPS compounder where the exit multiple best captures the market's willingness to pay for scarce monopoly economics. The Revenue DCF provides a conservative floor.

## 12. Verdict: FAIRLY VALUED

**Fair Value: ~$1,124 | Current Price: $1,049.86 | Upside: +7.0%**

FICO is priced within a reasonable range of fair value. The stock has pulled back significantly from its 2024 highs (~$2,000), and the market now implies only ~10% EPS growth — well below the company's 10-year track record of 26% median growth. The blended DCF suggests modest upside of 7%, driven by the base case view that FICO can sustain 15% EPS growth through continued score pricing power and operating leverage. However, the wide divergence between EPS DCF ($1,284) and Revenue DCF ($552) in the base case reflects genuine uncertainty about how to value a business that relies heavily on debt-funded buybacks and carries $3B in net debt against negative book equity. The EPS DCF captures FICO's monopoly franchise value; the Revenue DCF captures the leverage risk. At current prices, investors are getting the franchise at a fair price — not a bargain, but not overextended.

**Upside risks:**
- Score price increases continue at 10%+ annually with no regulatory pushback, sustaining revenue growth above 12%
- International expansion of FICO Score adoption in European and Asian lending markets (currently negligible)
- AI/analytics platform gains traction, creating a second high-margin growth engine alongside Scores

**Downside risks:**
- CFPB or Congressional action to regulate or cap FICO Score pricing — the single largest risk to the thesis
- VantageScore (owned by the three credit bureaus) gains market acceptance, eroding FICO's monopoly premium
- Rising interest expense on $3.1B of debt constrains buyback capacity if refinancing occurs at higher rates

**Conviction: Medium.** The monopoly quality of the business is unambiguous (67% ROIC, 46% margins), but the 132% divergence between DCF methods and the unusual capital structure (negative equity, debt-funded buybacks) reduce confidence in any single point estimate. The reverse DCF's implied 10.3% growth provides a useful anchor — FICO is priced for moderate deceleration, and moderate upside exists if the franchise sustains its recent momentum.
