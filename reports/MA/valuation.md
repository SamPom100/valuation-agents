## 1. Company Overview

Mastercard is one of the world's two dominant payment networks (alongside Visa), operating an asset-light "toll-booth" model that processes trillions of dollars in card and digital payment transactions globally. The company earns fees on every transaction routed through its network, generating extraordinary economics: 57.6% operating margins, 91% ROIC, and minimal capital requirements. Revenue has grown from $10.8B (2016) to $32.8B (2025), an ~13% CAGR, driven by the secular shift from cash to digital payments. Mastercard returns virtually all earnings to shareholders — $14.5B in buybacks and dividends in FY2025 alone on $15.0B of net income.

```
  Risk-free rate: 4.38% (10Y Treasury)
  Beta: 0.84
  ERP: 3.87% (S&P 500 trailing earnings yield)
```

CAPM of 7.62% is used for the EPS DCF (cost of equity for equity cash flows). WACC of 7.42% is used for the Revenue DCF (cost of capital for firm-level cash flows). The two are nearly identical because Mastercard carries minimal financial leverage (4.2% debt weighting). The low beta (0.84) reflects MA's defensive, non-cyclical earnings profile — payment volumes are resilient even in downturns.

## 12. Verdict: UNDERVALUED

**Fair Value: ~$715 | Current Price: $501.55 | Upside: +42.6%**

Mastercard appears significantly undervalued. The current price implies only ~3.5% EPS growth — essentially no real growth — which is dramatically inconsistent with a company that has compounded EPS at 18% annually for a decade and sits at the center of a secular shift from cash to digital payments. Even the bear case (10% EPS growth, 28x terminal P/E) yields a fair value 9% above the current price, suggesting limited downside. The base case of 14% EPS growth (a meaningful deceleration from the historical 18% CAGR) implies 41% upside. Mastercard's 91% ROIC, 50%+ FCFF margins, and duopoly market position make it one of the highest-quality businesses in public markets.

**Upside risks:**
- Emerging market digital payment adoption accelerates faster than expected (India, Africa, Southeast Asia still heavily cash-based)
- B2B payment flows ($120T+ addressable market) become a material revenue contributor through Mastercard Track and similar platforms
- Value-added services (cybersecurity, data analytics, consulting) grow faster than core network fees, expanding margins further

**Downside risks:**
- Regulatory intervention on interchange fees expands globally (EU-style caps in more jurisdictions)
- Real-time account-to-account payment rails (UPI in India, Pix in Brazil, FedNow in the US) gain traction as card alternatives
- Macro slowdown reduces consumer spending and cross-border transaction volumes, compressing growth below 10%

**Conviction: High.** Both DCF methods independently value MA above the current price across all scenarios. The cross-method divergence is moderate (26.6% in base case). The reverse DCF confirms the market is pricing in near-zero growth, which is inconsistent with every observable fundamental. The only scenario where MA is overvalued is one where the payment network model is structurally disrupted — a low-probability tail risk given the duopoly's decades-long track record of adapting to new payment technologies.
