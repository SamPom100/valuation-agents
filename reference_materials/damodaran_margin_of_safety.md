## Margin of Safety: Five Myths

From the May 2016 post:

Damodaran does NOT use a formal margin of safety in his investing process, though he acknowledges its potential value.

### Myth 1: MOS is costless
Increasing MOS reduces false positives (overpaying) but **increases false negatives** (missing bargains). This trade-off must generate positive net returns to justify the approach. A very large MOS means you rarely buy anything.

### Myth 2: Sloppy valuations are acceptable with MOS
"If your valuations are incomplete, badly done or biased, having a MOS on that value will provide little protection." A 25% margin of safety on a garbage valuation is still garbage.

### Myth 3: Uniform MOS across investments
**A flat 15% or 25% MOS doesn't account for different risk profiles.** A utility with stable cash flows needs less MOS than a startup. The appropriate MOS depends on:
- Valuation uncertainty (how confident you are in your estimate)
- Portfolio concentration (single stock vs diversified)
- Market efficiency (liquid large-cap vs illiquid small-cap)
- Pricing catalysts (what triggers price convergence to value)

### Myth 4: Portfolio MOS equals individual stock MOS
Diversification reduces the required MOS on individual holdings through the law of large numbers. A diversified portfolio of slightly undervalued stocks can outperform a concentrated portfolio of deeply undervalued stocks.

### Myth 5: MOS replaces risk measures
MOS is an add-on to valuation risk adjustment, not an alternative to discount rates. You still need proper risk modeling — MOS is additional protection on top.

### Damodaran's Own Approach
Rather than prescriptive MOS thresholds, he recommends:
- Sound intrinsic valuations as the foundation
- Flexible MOS tailored to uncertainty, concentration, efficiency, and catalysts
- Testing portfolio performance: excessive cash holdings or underperformance versus index funds signal problems

**Example:** He bought Valeant at $32 when his valuation yielded $45 — a 40% margin — but emphasizes judgment over rigid rules.

## Scenario Analysis: The 3P Framework

From the March 2026 AI post:

Damodaran advocates for probabilistic approaches over simple point estimates. His preferred framework categorizes scenarios into three tiers:

1. **Possible:** Can occur but lacks sufficient backing to assign high probability
2. **Plausible:** Has stronger foundational support — reasonable and grounded
3. **Probable:** Can be assigned realistic likelihood estimates

**Key principle:** "Scenarios without probability estimates are not useful" for decision-making.

### Mapping Two Dimensions
For any disruptive force (he uses AI as the example), map outcomes across:
- **Magnitude:** How severe is the impact?
- **Speed:** How quickly does it unfold?

Net effects are greatest when disruption is rapid and widespread. Extended timelines allow adjustment and reduce negative impacts.

3. **Valuation quality > MOS.** Per Damodaran, the project's extensive verification phase (Phase 7) is more valuable than any margin of safety threshold. Getting the valuation right matters more than the buffer.
